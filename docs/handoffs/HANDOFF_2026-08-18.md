## 1. THE HEADLINE — the ensemble-size crossover

Reliability weighting does not fail. **It works at small ensembles and stops working
at large ones**, monotonically. Every prior null in this project was measured at n=5
or n=8 — on the wrong side of the crossover.

QAMPARI, 777 questions, all 210 ensembles of size 3–6 drawn from 8 agents,
thresholds cross-fitted:

```
  size  best single   count   weighted   count-best   WEIGHTED-COUNT
    3       24.72      22.15    24.40      -2.57         +2.25
    4       26.00      23.69    25.59      -2.31         +1.90
    5       26.77      26.17    26.56      -0.60         +0.40
    6       27.18      27.74    27.38      +0.56         -0.36

  how often each SIGNIFICANTLY beats the other
  size   count > best single      weighted > count
    3          2/56                 39/56  (70%)
    4          8/70                 39/70  (56%)
    5         15/56                 20/56  (36%)
    6        11/28                   7/28  (25%)
```

Two monotone trends running opposite ways. Weighting matters most at n=3; counting
only starts beating the best single model at n=6.

`corr(relative dominance, weighted - count) = +0.488` over all 210 ensembles — the
mechanism now replicates **within QAMPARI alone**, without needing a second benchmark.

### 1.1 It survives every control (`analysis/crossover_controls.py`)

The dangerous alternative was estimation noise: at n=3 you fit 3 weights, at n=6 you
fit 6, from the same data — more parameters, noisier estimates, which alone predicts
decay. Ruled out.

```
  GAIN OVER COUNTING
  size    wgt-cf   wgt-ORACLE    pat-cf   pat-ORACLE
    3      +2.25      +2.26       +2.73      +2.77
    4      +1.90      +1.99       +2.57      +2.76
    5      +0.40      +0.52       +1.13      +1.51
    6      -0.36      -0.24       +0.36      +0.94
```

- **Oracle weights (fitted on the test set, zero estimation error) show the same
  decay.** Not an artefact.
- **The pattern-lookup ceiling crosses over too** (+2.77 → +0.94). The *available
  information* genuinely shrinks with ensemble size. That is the mechanism: with
  enough models the support COUNT becomes a sufficient statistic and model identity
  stops carrying anything.
- **uniform ≡ count identically on all 210 ensembles.** Harness verified. (score =
  w·count is monotone in count, so it can only realise the count filters.)
- cf ≈ oracle throughout, so our estimator is already near-optimal.

### 2.1 QAMPARI — settled

`P(correct | k agents assert)`: 1.2 → 11.1 → 17.4 → 27.2 → 36.5 → 46.3 → 51.9 → 56.5%.

Solo precision (8-agent pool) is much worse than the earlier 9.6% figure, which was
the 5-model pool at n=198:

```
  grok-4.3 5.7%   gpt-5.4-mini 2.6%   MAI 2.4%   gpt-5.4 2.0%
  Kimi 1.2%   Cohere 0.5%   DeepSeek 0.4%   gpt-5.4-nano 0.2%
```

against **11.1%** for *any* two agents agreeing. A solo claim is a strong signal of
being wrong.

`P(correct | exactly these two assert)` varies 12×, from MAI+nano 1.9% to MAI+grok
22.5% (sd 5.5). **So counting does discard real information** — but exploiting it
gains only +0.25 (ns) at n=8, because precision gains are offset by recall losses.
Information ≠ improvement. Worth stating in the paper.

### 2.2 ASQA — the metric was wrong, and fixing it changed the verdict

We had scored ASQA with **STR-EM**, which is recall-only, and concluded the benchmark
could not adjudicate a filter. STR-EM is one *component*. ASQA's headline is
**DR = geometric mean(ROUGE-L, Disambig-F1)**; the authors chose a geometric mean
explicitly "to penalize methods that maximize one metric at the cost of a significant
decrease in the other".

```
  arm                     DR*    STR-EM  ROUGE-L   words
  best single (gpt-5.4)  27.03    44.23   22.55      78
  union (all 6)          24.60    54.82   13.84     454
  count                  23.98    43.19   20.06
  uniform (control)      23.98    43.19   20.06     <- ≡ count, as required
  marginal               27.45    46.01   22.04     +3.48 vs count *
  solo                   27.45    46.01   22.04     +3.48 *
  nb-full                27.32    45.62   22.11     +3.34 *
  pattern (ceiling)      27.37    45.71   22.10     +3.40 *
```

Under STR-EM the union wins by +12.15; under DR it **loses by −4.33**. ROUGE-L against
a ~2-sentence human reference is the precision/brevity term we wrongly claimed ASQA
lacked.

`DR*` substitutes STR-EM for Disambig-F1 on the authors' statement that the two measure
the same aspect. **A faithful DR needs the RoBERTa-SQuADv2 reader** — stand it up before
publishing an ASQA number.

**Decision taken 18 Aug: ASQA stays, DR accepted.** The supervisor distrusts ROUGE, but
under STR-EM alone every arm collapses to union and ASQA cannot test a filter at all.
There is no third option.

## 3. QUEST — dropped, and why (keep the finding)

Its gold is derived from Wikipedia category intersections and is badly incomplete.
Audited with a judge outside the agent pool, n=60 per group:

```
  items >=6 of 8 agents assert, QUEST marks WRONG   73.3% actually CORRECT
  items in gold            (judge calibration)      86.7%
  items 1 agent only       (false-positive control) 28.3%
```

The controls show the judge discriminates, so the 73.3% is real. Examples: *Iron Man*
for "sci-fi films from 2008", *The Suicide Squad* for "2020s superhero comedy" — right,
just absent from the source category.

Signature in the data: `P(correct | k agents)` is **non-monotone** — 27.7% at 7 agents,
dropping to **17.6%** at unanimity. QAMPARI rises monotonically to 56.5%.

This biases specifically **against consensus**, which is the arm we compare to, so the
QUEST weighting result was confounded. **Dropped 18 Aug.**

Keep the audit as a finding: *"inter-model consensus is a better correctness signal
than a widely-used benchmark's gold labels"* generalises to any benchmark with
auto-derived gold.

## 4. Weighting schemes tested (cross-fitted, `analysis/weighting_schemes.py`)

```
  scheme          QAMPARI(n=8)          ASQA
  count               29.35 (ref)      23.98 (ref)
  marginal            28.11  -1.25*    27.45  +3.48*
  solo                29.44  +0.09     27.45  +3.48*
  nb-assert           28.40  -0.95*    (n/a)
  nb-full             29.45  +0.10     27.32  +3.34*
  pattern (CEILING)   29.60  +0.25     27.37  +3.40*
```

- **marginal** = `P(correct | j asserts)` — what was deployed. Wrong conditional: the
  only decision weighting changes is whether to admit a SOLO claim, which needs
  `P(correct | j asserts AND nobody else does)`.
- **solo** = that conditional. Beats marginal on both benchmarks.
- **nb-full** adds omission as evidence (`log((1−TPR)/(1−FPR))` for silent agents) and
  beats nb-assert on both — silence is informative, and the deployed rule scores it zero.
- **pattern** = `P(correct | exact subset asserting)`, shrunk toward the count-level
  rate. **This upper-bounds every who-said-what rule**, including correlation-aware and
  omission-aware ones, because the pattern already determines who agreed and who was
  silent. Only question-dependent or claim-content weights escape it.

**Structurally dead:** OW log-odds (`log(x/(1−x))` is negative for every precision < 0.5,
so more agreement means less support; optimum disables the filter) and OW
response-selection (reproduces best-single exactly, +0.00 zero-width CI, because no two
open-ended responses coincide).

**LLM-judged clustering is a negative result**: loses ~6 F1 vs edit distance at every
model size (gpt-5.5 24.80, gpt-5.4-mini 24.94 vs 31.18). It merges distinct entities and
only the representative survives, so a false merge deletes a correct answer. Note the
axis confusion it exposed: alias poverty is a **prediction↔gold** problem; clustering
operates **prediction↔prediction**.

## 6. Documents that are now WRONG

- `HANDOFF_CURRENT.md` — says no second benchmark can adjudicate (false twice: QUEST
  existed, ASQA was mis-scored); says weighting is null at +0.08 (it is −1.25 under
  honest cross-fitting).
- `PROGRESS_2026-08-17.docx` — same three errors, plus it labels the count filter "ASC".
- Anything calling the count filter **ASC**. It is a multi-agent adaptation:
  published ASC (arXiv:2405.13131) draws m=50 samples from ONE model, tunes Θ on a
  validation set, and composes survivors with an LLM. Counting over agents measures
  inter-model agreement, not self-consistency. Relabelled to `MA-count` throughout the
  code. Per the 19 Aug meeting this arm **is the team's proposed contribution**, not a
  baseline we failed to beat.

## 7. Traps (see `docs/troubleshooting.md` for the full list)

- **A long-lived generator silently drops ~50% of calls.** A fresh process succeeds
  36/36 on identical inputs. `generate()` writes only `if txt:`, so drops vanish without
  trace and the progress line never moves. **Run generation in passes** and print
  coverage each pass — the drivers in `generation/` do this.
- **Threshold tuning on the evaluation set flips signs.** +0.08 → −1.25 on QAMPARI.
  Nothing tuned in-sample is a result.
- **`AZ.spend()` understates cost** — several models have no PRICE entry. Check
  `usage["unpriced"]`.
- **Piping a long run through `tail` hides it** — buffering meant a 57-minute run looked
  identical to a hung one. Use `python -u` and redirect.
- **QAMPARI gold ships no aliases for 97.2% of entries**, so grading is near-exact match
  and understates every arm by ~7 F1 uniformly. Disclose; do not silently relax.

## 8. What I would do next, in order

1. **ASQA ensemble sweep** (n=3→6, cached, free). If the crossover replicates on a
   second dataset with a different answer shape, atomic unit and metric, that is the
   paper's central figure. This is the single highest-value remaining experiment.
2. **Deploy the OW paper's model pool** once someone has run `az login`. Small models,
   cheap, and it puts us in the all-weak regime where the OW paper reports its largest
   gains (>9% with four weak models) — which the crossover predicts should be our best
   case too.
3. **Question-dependent weights** using `v2.jsonl`'s domain labels. This is the ONLY
   idea remaining that escapes the pattern ceiling, because it uses information outside
   the support pattern.
4. **LongWeave SALES_REPORT/1k**, 100 samples × 6 models, within the $30 cap.
5. **TriviaQA canonicalisation** — free, and it is the control where OW applies.

**The claim to defend:** *multi-model atomic self-consistency is a strong naive
baseline; reliability weighting improves on it in the small-ensemble regime (3–4
models) and becomes unnecessary by 6, because the support count becomes a sufficient
statistic — and relative model dominance predicts where on that curve you sit.*
